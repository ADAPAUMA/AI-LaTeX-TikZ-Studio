1. Tell us about yourself and your life before IBM SkillsBuild

My name is Adapa Uma Siva Sankar. I am doing my B.Tech in CSE with Data Science specialization at V.S.M. College Of Engineering, Ramachandrapuram. Before IBM SkillsBuild, most of my learning happened inside the classroom or through YouTube videos I searched on my own. I was genuinely interested in AI and software, but I never really knew where to start with the actual industry tools.

Honestly, one thing that used to bother me was that college teaches you concepts but rarely shows you how things actually work in a real project. I would read about things like cloud computing or AI models and think "okay, but what do I actually do with this?" That gap between theory and practice was frustrating. I also did not have money to pay for expensive courses, so I was always looking for something free and actually useful.

2. How did you find IBM SkillsBuild and what made you join?

I came across IBM SkillsBuild through a WhatsApp group in my college. Someone shared the link and I almost ignored it at first because I had seen many such programs before that ended up being just certificates with no real learning. But when I actually opened the platform and saw that it included IBM technologies, Generative AI, and hands-on activities, I decided to register the same day.

I won't lie — the first few days felt overwhelming. The topics were new and I was not sure I would be able to complete everything. But I told myself that feeling confused at the beginning is normal and kept going. I am glad I did not quit early.

3. What did you learn during the program?

I explored several topics I had never studied properly before — Generative AI, cloud computing, cybersecurity, quantum computing, and software development. But the part I personally found most useful was actually building my own project using IBM tools.

My project was an AI-Powered LaTeX TikZ Diagram Generator and Chatbot Studio. I used IBM Bob, IBM Watson Assistant, IBM Granite, and watsonx.ai to build it. There were moments where things broke and I had no idea why. I remember spending almost two days trying to fix a 401 authentication error from the watsonx.ai API before I finally figured out my project ID was wrong. That kind of debugging taught me more than any lecture could.

I also learned how to connect different tools together and make them work as a system, which is something I had never done before in college.

4. How has your life changed after the program?

The biggest change is my confidence. Before this, I used to think industry tools were meant for experienced people and that I needed to know a lot more before trying them. Now I know that is not true. I have actually built something with IBM Granite and watsonx.ai as a final year student, and that feels good.

I also have a real project on my GitHub now with actual commits, a live Streamlit app, and documentation. When I go for interviews or internship applications, I have something concrete to show instead of just listing subjects from my syllabus.

More than the skills, the program changed how I think about learning. I am not scared to open a new technology and try it anymore.

5. What would you tell someone hesitant to join?

I would say just register and see. You do not need to know everything before starting. I did not know much about IBM tools when I joined and I still managed to build a full working AI application by the end.

The only thing that actually matters is whether you are willing to sit with a problem when it does not work and figure it out. If you are, you will get a lot out of this program. If you are waiting until you feel "ready" — that day will not come on its own. Just start.
